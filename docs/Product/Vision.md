# Recipe Assistant Product Vision

## 1. Product Overview

Recipe Assistant is a mobile cooking assistant that transforms recipe information into a structured and interactive cooking experience.

Instead of forcing users to repeatedly watch videos or search through recipe posts while cooking, the application organizes ingredients, preparation tasks, and cooking steps into a clear workflow that is easy to follow.

The first version will be released as a WeChat Mini Program. It will support both manually created recipes and recipe import, providing a simpler and more standardized cooking experience.

---

## 2. Background

Many people learn cooking from online videos or social-media recipes. These videos are excellent for demonstrating cooking techniques, but they are difficult to use during the actual cooking process.

Personally, I often cook by following video recipes. During cooking, I frequently need to pause the video, drag the progress bar backwards, or search for ingredient quantities and seasoning ratios that appeared several minutes earlier. Important information is scattered throughout the video instead of being available when it is needed.

This experience inspired the idea of Recipe Assistant: an application that converts scattered recipe information into a clear, structured cooking workflow.

---

## 3. Problem Statement

People who cook with video-based recipes cannot easily access all relevant information at the moment when they need it.

Important information is often distributed across the video, including:

- ingredient quantities;
- marinade ingredients;
- sauce ingredients;
- preparation instructions;
- cooking sequence;
- heat level;
- cooking duration;
- visual completion indicators.

As a result, users need to touch their phones repeatedly, search through the video timeline, and remember information that appeared earlier. This interrupts the cooking process and increases the risk of missing ingredients or using incorrect quantities.

Video recipes are optimized for watching, but not for cooking.

---

## 4. Target Users

### Primary User

The initial primary user are people who regularly cook by following online video recipes and wants a more structured way to use recipe information while cooking.

For the first version, the product will primarily be validated through the developer's own cooking workflow.

---

### Possible Future Users

- beginners who need detailed cooking guidance;
- people who save recipes from social-media platforms;
- users who want to adjust recipe quantities;
- users who want to estimate ingredient amounts with their own kitchen utensils;
- users who track calories or nutritional intake.

---

## 5. Product Vision

Make online recipes easier to prepare and follow by transforming scattered cooking information into a clear, step-by-step cooking workflow.

---

## 6. Initial Success Criteria

The first version of the product will be considered successful if it can achieve the following goals:

- A user can complete an entire recipe without repeatedly watching the original video.
- All ingredients, seasonings, and preparation tasks are visible before cooking starts.
- Cooking instructions are presented one step at a time.
- Recipes can be created manually with a simple and intuitive workflow.
- The developer can successfully replace the original recipe video during daily cooking.
- The application can be used comfortably with one hand during cooking.

---

## 7. Out of Scope

The following features are intentionally excluded from the first version:

- User accounts and social features.
- Recipe sharing between users.
- AI video recognition and automatic recipe generation.
- Nutrition and calorie analysis.
- Shopping lists.
- Meal planning.
- Recommendation algorithms.
- Multi-language support.
- Cloud synchronization.
- Payment and subscription features.

These features may be considered in future releases after the core cooking workflow has been validated.

---

## 8. Open Questions

- Should recipes be created manually, generated by AI, or support both approaches?
- How should spoon-size calibration be designed?
- Should recipes support adjustable serving sizes?
- Should the first version require user accounts?
- How should recipes be stored and synchronized?
- What is the minimum feature set required for the MVP?
- Should users be able to edit imported recipes?
- Should recipes remain private by default?

--- 

## 9. Product Principles

- Keep the interface simple.
- Minimize user interactions during cooking.
- Make important information visible before it is needed.
- Reduce unnecessary scrolling.
- Allow users to focus on cooking instead of operating the application.
